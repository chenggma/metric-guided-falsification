# M2: shaping is necessary, but only the potential-based form works

7 arms x 5 seeds x 100k env steps, SUT-A (IDM+MOBIL), scenario family F1.
Identical PPO hyperparameters everywhere; the reward is the only manipulated
variable. Held-out evaluation: 200 episodes per run, deterministic policy,
the same seed stream (master 90001) for every arm and for the random-OU
floor. 35/35 runs completed, 0 failures, 8.4 h wall clock at `--jobs 2`.

A failure counts only if it is **severe** (closing speed >= 5 m/s) **and**
SUT-attributable (fault label). Both gates are in the reward and in the
analysis; see falsify/fault.py for why the severity floor exists and
DESIGN.md for the amendment that introduced it mid-experiment.

## Result

| Arm | per-seed severe failures / 200 | median | timeout share (late) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **pora / PBRS** | **[10, 7, 26, 14, 13]** | **13** | 0.0% |
| **neg_tts / PBRS** | [9, 5, 9, 16, 0] | 9 | 0.0% |
| **inv_ttc / PBRS** | [8, 7, 13, 13, 1] | 8 | 0.0% |
| inv_ttc / raw | [0, 1, 0, 0, 0] | 0 | 84.3% |
| neg_tts / raw | [0, 0, 0, 0, 1] | 0 | 86.2% |
| pora / raw | [0, 0, 0, 0, 0] | 0 | 95.4% |
| sparse (control) | [0, 0, 0, 0, 0] | 0 | 0.0% |
| random-OU (floor) | [1] | 1 | - |

Three findings, in order of how much they survive scrutiny.

**1. Dense shaping is necessary.** The sparse-reward adversary found *zero*
severe failures on all five seeds - worse than random OU noise, which
stumbled into one. With the outcome gate closed properly, "crash severely"
is too rare an event for 100k steps of credit assignment to reach. Note
this reverses the pilot's headline (sparse 15/200), and the reason is
mechanical: under the pilot's leaky gate the sparse arm could also be paid
for cheap low-speed contacts, which made its reward far less sparse than
the label suggested.

**2. Only the potential-based form delivers it.** Every raw-shaped arm sits
at ~0 while every PBRS arm finds failures on 4 of 5 seeds. The raw arms are
not merely worse - they are farming: 84-95% of late-training episodes end
in timeout, collecting 24-70 of shaped reward for hovering and never
finishing the attack. Under PBRS the same metrics produce 0.0% timeouts and
*negative* timeout returns (-3.3, -4.6), which is what the telescoping sum
predicts and is a direct check that PBRS is behaving as theory says.

**3. The dose-response holds.** Farmability still tracks metric density:
pora 95.4% > neg_tts 86.2% > inv_ttc 84.3% timeout share, the same order as
in the pilot. And under PBRS the ordering *inverts into benefit* - pora,
the densest signal and the worst farmer, becomes the best falsifier
(median 13, never below 7, the only arm with no near-zero seed).

## Statistics, including a design error I have to own

Exact Mann-Whitney over per-seed values, Holm-adjusted across the nine
contrasts registered in DESIGN.md:

| Contrast | p | Holm |
|---|---|---|
| sparse vs inv_ttc/PBRS | **0.0079** | 0.0714 |
| sparse vs pora/PBRS | **0.0079** | 0.0714 |
| pora: raw vs PBRS | **0.0079** | 0.0714 |
| inv_ttc: raw vs PBRS | 0.0159 | 0.0952 |
| sparse vs neg_tts/PBRS | 0.0476 | 0.2381 |
| neg_tts: raw vs PBRS | 0.0476 | 0.2381 |
| sparse vs each raw arm | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |

Three contrasts hit **0.0079 - the exact floor of the design**, meaning
perfect separation: no arrangement of five-vs-five values could be more
extreme. And none survive Holm at 0.05.

That is not a fact about the results; it is a fact about the plan. With
n = 5 the smallest attainable two-sided p is 2/C(10,5) = 0.0079, and Holm
multiplies the smallest by the number of contrasts: 9 x 0.0079 = 0.0714.
**The registered analysis could not have rejected at 0.05 no matter what
the data did** - computable before a single run, and I did not compute it.
The seed budget and the correction scheme were specified independently and
were mutually incompatible.

The fix is arithmetic, not interpretive: at n = 6 the floor is 0.00216
(Holm 0.0195) and at n = 7 it is 0.00058 (Holm 0.0052). DESIGN.md §8
registered "**>= 5** seeds per arm", so extending is within protocol rather
than a change to it, and it is applied uniformly to all seven arms rather
than to the arms that happen to be winning. Seeds 6-7 are running; this
section will be restated with n = 7 and the seed-5 numbers kept above so
both are visible. Readers should weigh that the extension decision was
made after seeing the n = 5 p-values, even though its justification does
not depend on them.

## Diversity and transfer

Distinct failure-mode cells (closing speed x |relative heading| x ego
speed), union across seeds: pora/PBRS 10, neg_tts/PBRS 10, inv_ttc/PBRS 8,
every raw arm 1. The PBRS arms do not just find more failures, they find
structurally different ones.

Cross-metric transfer, median episode-max metric on each arm's own
failures: the raw arms' handful of successes sit at inverse-TTC 10.0 - the
cap - while PBRS-arm failures sit at 3.3-4.1. When a farming policy does
crash, it crashes in the most TTC-extreme way available, consistent with
being driven by that signal; it just almost never crashes.

## What this does not establish

* One SUT (IDM+MOBIL) and one scenario family (straight highway). SUT-B
  (learned PPO ego) and the intersection family are M3.
* 100k steps per run. A larger budget might let the sparse arm eventually
  find the signal; the claim is about this budget, which is held identical
  across arms.
* PORA here is PORA-under-constant-velocity-Gaussian-occupancy, as in
  risk-metric-bench; these numbers do not grade the original paper's
  learned predictor.
* The severity floor is a proxy for harm, not a validated injury model.

`scripts/run_m2.py --seeds 7 --jobs 2`, then
`scripts/analyze_m2.py results/m2`. Runs are resumable and seed-major, so
